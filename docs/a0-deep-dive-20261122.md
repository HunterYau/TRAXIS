## Confirmed comparison defect and repair

`compare()` previously used only `np.interp` to move simulated voltage from
approximately 23.4 MS/s to a 5 MS/s comparison grid. This is point sampling with
linear interpolation, not an adequate anti-aliasing low-pass stage. A 5.23 MHz
component can fold toward 230 kHz on that grid, close to the excitation carrier.
This can corrupt an apparent low-frequency waveform difference.

Added `examples/pzt_sampling.py` with a rational polyphase FIR resampler. The
ratio is derived from the actual time spacing; no signal-based gain, phase or
delay fitting occurs. The filter is a fixed Kaiser-beta-10 FIR with zero-padded
boundaries. Added `sampling_band_comparison` to the experimental comparison and
labelled results in the text console. Both measured and simulated traces pass
through the sampling helper; an already-5-MS/s record is unchanged.

Original comparison values and full-band convergence results are retained.
The convergence assessment now prints both raw full-band and defined sampled-band
changes/orders. Existing full-record comparisons, arrival flags and saved voltages
were not rewritten. All five saved run-010 comparisons were recomputed into a new
analysis folder and every pre-existing comparison field matched its saved value.

This is a defined common-band comparison, not a claim that the NI digitizer used
this exact filter. Actual acquisition/decimation/filter settings were not recorded
in the information inspected. A 5 MS/s stored record alone does not prove its
analog input had no frequencies above 2.5 MHz. Finite FIR rolloff and boundary
effects remain. Neither a filtered score nor the low-pass diagnostic establishes
full-band numerical convergence or real-world accuracy.

## Likely excitation mechanism; not yet proven in the full system

The simulator reconstructs the stored generator voltage with straight segments.
Spectral analysis of that reconstructed 250 kHz drive finds an image in the
5.1–5.4 MHz range at -52.60 dB power relative to the 150–350 kHz carrier band.
Its coincidence with the troublesome receiver spectrum is a strong lead. It is
not a causal proof: PZT/plate higher-frequency modes and transient excitation
can also contribute. No full-system source-reconstruction A/B test was run.

Added an explicit candidate FIR reconstruction utility in
`examples/pzt_drive_reconstruction.py`. In an input-waveform-only check, its
image/carrier power ratio is -118.29 dB, a reduction of 65.69 dB. It uses the
stored drive samples only; no receiver trace is fitted. The utility is not enabled
in production or in the stopped convergence audit. Its zero-phase FIR is not a
calibrated causal DAC/amplifier model and has boundary ringing. Actual NI generator
filter settings and actuator-terminal voltage remain unknown, so it cannot be
asserted to reproduce the original experiment.

## Research on wave physics and equipment

1. **Generator reconstruction.** NI documents digital interpolation/image rejection
   for the PXIe-5413 and explicitly distinguishes filter-enabled operation from
   operation without reconstruction image rejection. The actual experiment's
   generator configuration is unknown. Treating straight-line interpolation as
   the known physical output is not justified.
   https://www.ni.com/docs/en-US/bundle/pxie-5413-specs/page/specs.html
   https://www.ni.com/en/shop/electronic-test-instrumentation/waveform-generators/generating-signals-function-generators.html

2. **Amplifier.** The Krohn-Hite 7500 manufacturer manual specifies about -3 dB
   at 1 MHz and increasing phase lag, reaching about 100 degrees at 1 MHz, under
   its stated specification conditions. It has frequency-dependent source
   impedance and load/current limits. The current ideal unit-peak model omits
   this transfer. These specifications do not establish its response under the
   particular capacitive PZT load, especially at 5 MHz; a guessed one-pole filter
   or a fitted delay is not an experimentally established correction.
   https://krohn-hite.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/7500_man.pdf

3. **Digitizer.** NI specifies selectable 50-ohm/1-megohm input paths and an
   optional 24 MHz anti-alias filter for the PXIe-5105. Its high-rate ADC and the
   stored 5 MS/s waveform are different stages. The 24 MHz filter alone does not
   protect arbitrary subsequent 5 MS/s downsampling from aliasing. Input loading,
   cable capacitance, bandwidth, noise and range matter. Model name alone does
   not identify the experiment's settings.
   https://www.ni.com/docs/en-US/bundle/pxie-5105-specs/page/specs.html

4. **PZT loss and resonance.** The manufacturer reports NCE51 Qm=80 and dielectric
   dissipation factor 0.015. The active PZT tensors are lossless. Mechanical,
   dielectric and coupling losses affect phase and ringing, as described in
   COMSOL's piezoelectric loss theory. Omitting them can prolong high-frequency
   ringing. A catalog Q does not uniquely determine broadband damping, especially
   for the bonded sensor or a different resonance; none was invented here.
   https://www.ctscorp.com/Product-Series/NCE51.htm
   https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.sme/sme_ug_modeling.05.109.html

5. **Wave sensing and mode cancellation.** Sensor dimensions and wavelength affect
   sensitivity. Published research describes transducer-size tuning and suppression
   of selected modes. Top/bottom subtraction amplifies the relative importance of
   small mismatches when the remaining signal is weak. Dispersion, reflected and
   converted modes, bond asymmetry and sensor-body scattering are additional
   plausible contributors to real-world disagreement.
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5580041/
   https://research.cec.sc.edu/files/lamss/files/j123_jimss_2018_mei_giurgiutiu.pdf

6. **Wandowski data scope.** The paper explicitly reports A0 in the *subtracted*
   PZT signal up to 140 kHz, with the analogous FBG result up to 150 kHz. This
   supports retaining the high-frequency A0 identification warning. It is a
   reported detection result, not a universal PZT operating cutoff or proof that
   every later oscillation is absent. The same paper discusses solder mass,
   asymmetric wrap-around electrodes and glue as unresolved sensor differences.
   https://zenodo.org/records/18937842
   Local extracted paper: `outputs/experimental-references/experimental-validation-20261116/sources/mode-separation-preprint.pdf.txt`.

7. **Resampling method.** SciPy documents upsampling, low-pass FIR filtering and
   downsampling for `resample_poly`, including boundary assumptions.
   https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.resample_poly.html

Instrumentation settings cannot explain differences between otherwise identical
numerical runs unless they enter the numerical input/model. The confirmed issue
in this investigation is the comparison's missing anti-alias stage; the proposed
source-image/ringing mechanism and the real-world phase mismatch require further
controlled evidence.
